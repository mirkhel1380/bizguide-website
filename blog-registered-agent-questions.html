Registered agents

Seven questions to ask a registered agent service

Review document delivery, address use, renewal terms and support before choosing a registered agent.

b
By BizGuidePublished 19 October 2026 · 2 min read
IN THIS ARTICLEStart with the roleAsk these seven questionsTest the practical detailsKeep the relationship organizedSources & further reading← All articles
THE KEY IDEA

Compare the actual service agreement. An address, a dashboard and a compliance reminder can be separate services.

Start with the role

A registered agent receives official and legal documents for a business. The SBA explains that an agent must be located in the state where the business registers. The exact requirements should be checked with that state’s filing office.

Use that basic role as the starting point for your comparison. Ask each provider to describe the included work in writing. A clear answer is more useful than a long list of promotional features. Make a separate note of any service that requires an extra subscription.

Ask these seven questions

  1. Which entity and state does this order cover? Check the legal company name and the service start date.
  2. How will I receive a document? Ask about scanning, alerts, access and handling of originals.
  3. Which uses of the address are permitted? Ask separately about official filings, ordinary mail and public marketing.
  4. What will renewal cost? Request the recurring price and billing date, not just the first-year offer.
  5. Are reminders included? Confirm whether a reminder also includes preparation or filing. Do not assume it does.
  6. How do I cancel or change providers? Ask what action you must take with the state and whether separate fees apply.
  7. Who can resolve a problem? Find out how support handles a missed alert, account-access issue or document question.

Test the practical details

Build a small comparison sheet with one row for each question. Mark answers as confirmed, unclear or not included. If a provider gives a verbal answer that matters to your decision, request a written reference to the relevant terms.

Think through an ordinary workday: you are away from your desk, a document arrives, and someone needs to see it promptly. Which email address gets the alert? Who has permission to open the account? What happens if that person is unavailable? These planning questions can reveal a weak handover before you buy.

Keep the relationship organized

Once you choose a service, save the agreement, receipt and renewal date in the company’s records. Keep your notification details current and decide who checks the dashboard. Review the scope when your business changes rather than assuming the original arrangement still fits.

For an explanation of the role before you compare services, read what a registered agent does. Confirm a provider’s current price and service terms directly before ordering.
