Your first business website: domain, hosting and access

Map the services behind your website and make sure your business can access, renew and maintain them.

THE KEY IDEA

Keep a clear record of who controls the domain, website files, DNS and email. Plan the handover before someone builds the site.

Map the services before buying

A domain is the address people use to find your website; hosting serves the website’s content. Email and website-building tools may be separate services. A bundle can be convenient, but write down what is included so you can compare it fairly.

ICANN explains that a domain registration agreement governs matters such as fees, renewals and transfers. Read that agreement alongside the advertised offer. Ask which organization is the registrar if the seller acts as a reseller.

Put account access in the project brief

Before a designer or helper starts work, agree how account ownership and access will be handled. Your business should know which account holds the domain, where recovery messages go, and how an authorized person can make changes. Use separate authorized access where the service supports it rather than casually sharing a password.

Make an inventory with a service name, account owner, recovery contact, renewal date and a note explaining its purpose. Store passwords and recovery material securely outside that ordinary inventory. Include a handover checklist in the project scope so access does not become an afterthought.

Define a useful first version

For a small informational site, start with a clear home page, a useful explanation of your services, an About page and a working contact route. Describe what visitors can actually do. If a button says “contact,” it should lead to a real contact method; if it says “read,” it should open useful content.

  • Check headings and navigation on a small screen.
  • Open every important link and test the contact email.
  • Read the text as a first-time visitor.
  • Check the spelling of your business name and domain.
  • Remove unfinished forms, placeholder claims and empty sections.

Prepare for updates and renewals

Ask how to save a copy of your content, who can publish a change and what happens if you change providers. Keep your own copy of the latest approved text and design assets. Review access whenever a collaborator leaves the project.

Create reminders for domain and hosting renewals and confirm the recovery email remains accessible. A modest website you can maintain is a better starting point than an elaborate one whose accounts you cannot manage. For a broader checklist, visit our online presence planning guide.
