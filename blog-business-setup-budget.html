Planning & costs

Build a business setup budget beyond the headline price

Separate initial costs, renewals, optional services and unknown expenses in a simple comparison worksheet.

By BizGuidePublished 19 October 2026 · 2 min read

THE KEY IDEA

Compare what you will pay now and what you may need to pay later. A short written scope is more useful than a low headline price.

Create four budget columns

Use four columns: item, due date, amount and evidence. Under evidence, save a link to the current quote or official fee page. Keep government charges separate from a provider’s service fee so you can tell what you are paying for.

The SBA’s startup-cost guidance distinguishes one-time expenses from recurring expenses. Apply that idea to your setup worksheet: one section for getting started and another for keeping the business operating. Leave an amount blank when it has not been confirmed instead of treating it as zero.

Compare equivalent scopes

Two packages can have the same price and cover different work. Ask whether each includes preparation, submission, document delivery and support for a rejected filing. Compare the same state, entity type and delivery preference. Keep optional upgrades visible rather than folding them into a single unexplained total.

Budget groupQuestions to record
Initial setupWhich filing and service charges are due now?
Recurring servicesWhat renews, when, and at which price?
OperationsWhich website, email, accounting or other tools will you actually use?
Unconfirmed itemsWho will confirm the requirement and amount?

Use an illustrative comparison

Imagine two fictional quotes. Quote A is 100 units initially and 120 units for the next service year. Quote B is 160 units initially and 40 units for the next service year. For those two periods alone, A totals 220 and B totals 200. These invented figures illustrate a comparison method; they are not current market prices or a complete business budget.

Now ask what those totals exclude. A useful worksheet makes gaps obvious. Add a note for cancellation conditions, taxes, optional services and charges that still need confirmation. Never compare totals without comparing the underlying work.

Give every renewal an owner

For each recurring item, record a person responsible for reviewing it and a date to make a decision before renewal. Keep the original purchase date, the next billing date and the cancellation instructions together. Review usage before adding another subscription.

Finish with two lists: confirmed commitments and unresolved questions. This keeps estimates from quietly becoming assumptions. See our costs and renewals overview for the difference between provider fees and state requirements.
