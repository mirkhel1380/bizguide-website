Domain renewal checklist: keep your business address under control

A practical routine for checking domain access, renewal dates, billing details and recovery contacts before expiration.

THE KEY IDEA

A domain renewal is an ongoing responsibility. Give it an owner, a recorded date and a verified payment route before it becomes urgent.

Start with the account, not the reminder email

Your domain may be managed separately from your website and email. Start by signing in through the registrar’s known website and confirming the domain appears in the account. An invoice in your inbox is not a complete account record, and a working website does not tell you when its domain expires.

Record the registrar, exact domain spelling, expiration date and the person responsible for renewal. If a designer bought the domain, clarify who is the registered holder and how your business receives authorized account access. Resolve uncertainty while the site is working, rather than during an interruption.

Review the next renewal as a separate purchase

Check the renewal price and billing period shown in your account. A first-year promotional price is not a useful substitute for the next invoice. Confirm whether any email, privacy or hosting services have separate subscriptions and dates.

Auto-renewal is useful, but verify its actual status and the payment method attached to this domain. Check the card’s expiry and whether your provider reports a failed payment. Treat the renewal confirmation and updated expiration date as evidence that renewal completed.

Keep a small renewal record

A renewal record to complete in your own private workspace
RecordWhat to verify
Domain and registrarThe exact address and the account that manages it
Expiration and renewalCurrent expiration date, renewal period and expected cost
Responsible personWho checks payment and confirms the new date
Recovery contactAn accessible address approved for account recovery

Keep passwords, recovery codes and payment details out of this ordinary worksheet. Store sensitive access material securely. If the account offers separate authorized users, use that capability rather than passing a shared password around.

Set your own review dates

ICANN advises registrants to keep contact information current, pay on time and understand their registrar’s expiration terms. Make a calendar review before the renewal becomes urgent; for example, check the account a month ahead and confirm completion closer to the date. These are planning reminders, not a statement of the provider’s renewal rules.

Check that renewal messages reach a monitored inbox. Consider how you would recover access if email on this same domain stopped working. Review recovery options with the registrar before changing them, and keep a record of who approved the change.

If the domain has already expired

Open the registrar’s official account or support route promptly. Ask for the domain’s current status, available recovery options, full cost and any deadline that applies. Do not assume another provider follows the same grace period, or that every extension can be recovered on the same terms.

Do not pay an unexpected email invoice solely because it mentions your domain. Verify the request in your known account first. Once access is restored, confirm the website and email work and update your renewal record.
