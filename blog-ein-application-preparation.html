Business formation

Getting ready for an EIN application

Understand the IRS application route, prepare consistent information and keep your confirmation organized.

b
By BizGuidePublished 19 October 2026 · 2 min read

THE KEY IDEA

The IRS issues EINs without a government application fee. Check which application method you qualify for before starting.

Separate the number from a paid service

An employer identification number is a federal tax identifier. The IRS does not charge to issue one. If you pay a company to help with an application, ask it to identify its service fee separately and explain what assistance you are buying.

Do not treat a paid package as evidence that an application is approved. Keep a copy of the service description, any authorization and the information submitted. If you are unsure whether your situation requires an EIN, review the IRS guidance or ask a qualified tax professional.

Check your application route

The IRS online tool has eligibility requirements, including conditions about the business’s principal location and the responsible party’s taxpayer identification. Alternative routes exist for applicants who cannot use the online tool. Use the IRS page to confirm the route that fits your facts.

For a newly created LLC or corporation, the IRS says to form the entity with the state before applying. Prepare from the accepted formation record rather than a draft name you have not confirmed.

Make a consistent information sheet

Write a private review sheet before beginning. Put the company’s legal name at the top, then list the details the current IRS instructions request. Compare names and addresses across the records you will rely on. Do not invent a taxpayer number or choose a role you do not understand to get past a screen.

  • Note which source document supports each answer.
  • Write questions for the responsible party before starting.
  • Confirm any representative’s authority and the scope of their work.
  • Decide where the confirmation will be stored.
  • Keep personal identifiers out of public notes and shared marketing folders.

Review, save and hand over

After applying, keep the confirmation with your business records. Record who submitted the application and where supporting material is stored. If you discover an inconsistency, check the IRS correction instructions or obtain professional help instead of submitting another application automatically.

An EIN application is one administrative step. It does not replace a review of your tax obligations or the separate requirements of a bank or payment provider. Create a handover note for your accountant that identifies what has been completed and which questions remain open. Read our post-formation organization checklist for a practical records workflow.
