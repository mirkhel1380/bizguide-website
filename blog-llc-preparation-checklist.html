Before you form an LLC: a practical preparation checklist

Organize your business details, open questions and documents before you submit a formation order.

THE KEY IDEA

A clear brief makes a formation application easier to review. Start with what your business will do, where it will operate and who will own it.

Write a one-page business brief

Before comparing formation packages, describe your planned activity in plain English. Write down what you intend to sell, where work will happen, whether you expect employees, and who will make decisions. Treat this as a working brief, not a legal document. It helps you ask useful questions instead of selecting a package because its headline price looks attractive.

Add a short list of unresolved points. For example: will there be one owner or several, will anyone contribute equipment, and who will keep the company records? Bring these questions to an appropriate adviser before making commitments you do not understand.

Check the state and the business name

An LLC is formed under state law, and requirements differ between states. Start with the relevant state filing office rather than assuming one national checklist applies everywhere. If your business will operate across state lines, ask whether additional registration is necessary.

Create a shortlist of names, then distinguish a state entity-name search from a domain search and a trademark review. Those are different checks. Keep links to the official tools you use and record when you checked them. Avoid ordering printed branding until the important naming questions are resolved.

Prepare a document review sheet

  • Proposed company name and backup name.
  • Owners’ full legal names, with spelling checked against their records.
  • Questions about management and signing authority.
  • Addresses requested by the filing office, with the purpose of each confirmed.
  • Registered agent details and written confirmation of the service scope.
  • Copies of quotes, terms and any authorizations you sign.

Do not guess at a field just to finish a form. If an address or role is unclear, pause and ask the filing office or provider what the field requires. Keep sensitive documents in a secure location rather than sending them through a general website contact form.

Plan the next steps before submitting

A formation filing, federal tax identification and ongoing administration are separate tasks. IRS guidance also distinguishes an LLC’s state legal structure from its federal tax classification. Do not select a tax election simply because its name appears in an application menu.

Before you pay, write down who submits the filing, who checks the returned document, how corrections are handled and which tasks remain yours. Save the accepted document alongside your original order so you can compare them. For a wider overview, see our LLC planning guide.
