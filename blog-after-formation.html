After formation: organize your next 30 days

Turn a folder of company documents into a practical records, responsibility and follow-up system.

b
By BizGuidePublished 19 October 2026 · 2 min read
IN THIS ARTICLEDays 1–7: organize the recordsDays 8–14: assign the open questionsDays 15–21: build a reliable calendarDays 22–30: review the handoverSources & further reading← All articles
THE KEY IDEA

Use this as an organization plan, not a legal deadline calendar. Confirm the actual obligations and dates for your own business.

Days 1–7: organize the records

Create one secure place for the accepted formation document, internal agreements, receipts and correspondence. Give files descriptive names and decide who can access them. Compare the returned filing against the approved order and list any issue that needs follow-up.

Build a short company reference sheet with the legal name, formation state, filing reference and trusted contact details. Keep sensitive owner information separate. The purpose is to help the right person find an answer without searching through unrelated email threads.

Days 8–14: assign the open questions

Create a task list with four fields: question, responsible person, source and next action. Questions might concern tax setup, licenses, insurance, bookkeeping or permission to operate in another location. Assign a real person to obtain an answer rather than leaving a vague reminder that says “compliance.”

The SBA describes ongoing compliance as a combination of state, federal and other requirements that vary with the business. Use official authorities and qualified advisers to confirm which requirements apply. The 30-day structure here is only an editorial workflow; it does not determine any filing deadline.

Days 15–21: build a reliable calendar

Once a requirement has been confirmed, record its actual due date and the source of that date. Add an earlier preparation reminder. Distinguish government filing dates from provider subscription renewals: they may have different consequences and require different actions.

  • Write the exact name of the filing or renewal.
  • Record which authority or provider receives it.
  • Assign a person to prepare and another to review where practical.
  • Save evidence of submission and any acceptance.
  • Recheck dates when ownership, activity or location changes.

Days 22–30: review the handover

Walk through the system as if someone else had to take over tomorrow. Can they find the documents, identify the next task and contact the right adviser? Can an authorized person reach the service accounts? Fix missing access and unclear responsibility before expanding the tool list.

End with a short monthly review: completed tasks, upcoming dates, unanswered questions and costs to confirm. Keep a record of decisions as well as documents. This makes future conversations with providers and advisers more focused. For the money side of the plan, read how to build a setup budget.
